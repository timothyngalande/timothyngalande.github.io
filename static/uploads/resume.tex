\begin{rSubsection}{Geographical Segregation, Misallocation and Productivity in Apartheid South Africa Manufacturing}{}{PhD thesis chapter}{}
\item Quasi-experimental design using railway network upgrades as an instrument. Finds that state-led rail improvements causally increased manufacturing productivity through enhanced allocative efficiency, despite being designed to enforce spatial segregation.
\end{rSubsection}

\begin{rSubsection}{Quantifying Apartness: A General Equilibrium Estimation of the Cost of Labour Discrimination in South Africa}{}{PhD thesis chapter}{}
\item Calibrates a static general equilibrium model to quantify aggregate losses from the job reservation system. Counterfactual analysis shows the entire productivity cost of discrimination operated through misallocation, not technical inefficiency.
\end{rSubsection}

\begin{rSubsection}{Race-Biased Technological Progress and Growth in 20th Century Industrial South Africa}{}{PhD thesis chapter}{}
\item Growth accounting analysis of mining and manufacturing. Finds manufacturing growth was driven by technologically biased progress, while mining relied on intensification of cheap unskilled labour.
\end{rSubsection}
